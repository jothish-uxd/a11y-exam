## What each section covers (summary)

- **1.1** Medical, social, biopsychosocial, economic, functional solutions, social identity/cultural affiliation, and charity models, with strengths and weaknesses.
- **1.2** Categories of disability, the barriers each meets, and solutions.
- **1.3** Assistive technologies and adaptive strategies for permanent, temporary and episodic disabilities: visual, auditory, deaf-blindness, speech and language, mobility and body structure, cognitive, seizure, psychological, and multiple/complex.
- **1.4** Disability demographics, statistics and trends.
- **1.5** Disability etiquette in practice.
- **2.1** Individual accommodations compared with universal design.
- **2.2** Benefits of accessibility.
- **2.3** WCAG principles for web accessibility.
- **2.4** Accessibility principles for the built environment.
- **2.5** The principles of universal design.
- **2.6** Universal Design for Learning, usability and user experience.
- **3.1** Universal Declaration of Human Rights, CRPD, Marrakesh Treaty.
- **3.2** EU Charter of Fundamental Rights, African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights, Inter-American Convention on disability discrimination.
- **3.3** UK Equality Act 2010, ADA 1990, Ontarians with Disabilities Act 2001, disability laws in EU countries.
- **3.4** Domain-specific laws and government procurement laws.
- **3.5** Applying accessibility standards and regulations to ICT.
- **3.6** Integrating accessibility across an organization: resources, W3C WAI recommendations, maturity models, management champions, evaluation, recruiting and hiring, communication, legal and PR implications, procurement.
